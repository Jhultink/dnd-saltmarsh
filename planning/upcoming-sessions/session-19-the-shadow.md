# Session 19 Plan — The Shadow

**Picking up from:** The party escaped Lolth's sanctum with the compass, Hex, and Joss, and retreated to the Stormcaller. Lolth let them go. Hex promised he'd tell them more "once we're out of this cursed place."
**Session goal:** Sail into the storm, board the Shadow, and put the party in front of a choice where the only safe play is to touch nothing.

---

## Carried forward from Session 18

- **Compass** — points by web, not by north. Party has it.
- **Hex's promise** — he owes them an explanation. Collect on it in Scene 1.
- **Joss Tagrin** — aboard, not in fighting shape. Needs to get home eventually.
- **Snacks' death wish** — he tried to die against Lolth and was dragged out. Unexplained. The chair is a *very* attractive option for a PC in that state.
- **Short Stacks was tied up and Held** by his own party. Friction still live.
- **Xendros is still owed a magic item.**
- **Unfound lore** (Room 5, never reached) — still droppable anywhere: *breaking the curse doesn't destroy what's aboard the Shadow, it frees it.*

## The Truth — DM only

Not stated aboard the ship. Party gets it in Scene 5, from Seraphel or Xendros.

- **Purple orb** = a severed piece of **Lolth's power**, cut away when she took the Abyss. Sunk because the sea keeps things. Can't be destroyed.
- **Gold orb** = **Seraphel**, male Aasimar celestial. He *volunteered*. He didn't cast the seal — he became it. Awake, alone, centuries.
- **The storm** hides the ship and bars gods from reaching it. It's degrading.
- **The chair** is a mortal anchor, not a throne. Sitting puts a person into the binding's circuit. **Lolth takes whoever sits.**
- **First break already happened:** original captain **Emerich Sallow** sat, was taken. Chair filled and emptied — the journal's line.
- **Second break** = whoever sits next. Seraphel's grip fails, ship rips open, both orbs come free. Lolth's power walks out wearing the sitter's body; Seraphel is restored to a man.
- **Doing nothing is the win condition** — and it's unsatisfying by design. No riddle, no clever plan, no heroic sacrifice improves it.

### Ilvessa — who gave Hex the star chart

- A **drider**. Ruined drow priestess running Lolth's errands as ongoing penance. Offscreen this session.
- Met Hex three times, always seated in the dark. Leaving the third time, **he looked back and saw her move.** He took the chart anyway. He was 22.
- **He has known she's a drider for thirty years and never told anyone.** This shames him more than the chair does.
- **The chart is not a sky — it's a web.** Eight points, concentric rings. Lolth's sigil. It works because it's the same tech as the compass.
- What she told him — true except one word: *the chair is the only thing that will hold it.* She never said what happens to the person.
- **Then she broke ranks**, quiet, on his way out:

> *"If you ever get down there and there's a light that's kind to you — believe the light."*

He's never understood it and never forgotten it. He'll repeat it without knowing he's describing the gold orb. **Let the players connect it.**

### What Hex is hiding

- He's known about the chair for thirty years and **intends to sit in it.** He thinks this is noble.
- He's always known Ilvessa was a drider. This is the one he's ashamed of.
- **The blood oath in Session 15 was never about a prize** — it was about binding a competent crew to deliver him to that chair.

## Scene 1 — The Storm Parts

- **Setup:** Compass points. The permanent storm opens a corridor of flat water — walls of cloud standing off like something holding a door.
- Hex has fought this wall for thirty years and it has never given him an inch. Now it's letting them through.
- **This is where he pays off his promise.** At his own pace: there's a ship, there's an empty chair, the chair's been empty since the man who built the ship sat in it and never got up, and the ship is failing without someone in it.
- He does **not** volunteer the drider unless pushed. He does say he's going to want to do something they won't like, and he'd rather they hear it now while there's still time to talk him out of it.
- **Paths:**
  - If the PCs interrogate the source of his information → the third meeting, the thing under the table, "believe the light."
  - If the PCs try to talk him down here → he'll listen, won't commit. Let them extract a promise he may not keep.
  - If the PCs restrain or disarm him now → that's a real solution. It shifts the pressure onto *which PC sits instead.*
  - If they say nothing → he takes silence as permission.
- **Leaves behind:** the party knows a chair exists and that Hex wants it, before they ever see it.

## Scene 2 — Boarding the Shadow

- **Setup:** Empty, silent, a century too old in every detail. Rope that should have rotted, brass that never greened, deck worn smooth by feet that aren't there.
- **What's here:**
  - **The quarterdeck chair** — bolted behind the wheel. Every line on this ship is worn except that chair.
  - **The captain's name**, if they look: brass plate / ship's register / logbook flyleaf — ***Emerich Sallow, master.*** Nothing else of him survives. No portrait, no effects, no last entry.
  - **A dead man**, three steps from the port rail. Old bones, a **mate's** coat — not Sallow.

> *He's stretched out — reaching, one arm forward, fingers still curled around nothing. He was crawling toward the rail, and he did not make it.*

- **DC 15 Insight/Medicine:** he wasn't fleeing. He was trying to throw something *overboard* and died three steps short. He watched Sallow sit down and stop existing, and spent his last hours trying to put the compass into the sea.
- **Below decks** gets older and wronger; the ship is bigger inside than out. Compass keeps pulling **down** — past the hold, past the bilge, past where a ship stops having decks.
- **Paths:**
  - If the PCs find and read the mate's body → strongest possible warning, delivered without a word. Let them draw the line to the chair themselves.
  - If they search for records → Sallow's name and nothing else. The absence lands harder than a biography would.
  - If someone sits in the chair *here*, before the bottom → it works. Skip to Branch B. Don't stop them.

## Scene 3 — The Bottom of the Shadow

*This is the scene. Temptation, not exposition. Explain nothing here.*

> *Chains run out of the dark into every rib of the hull, hundreds of them, and where they converge they hold two things off the floor, locked against each other like hands. One is violet — it strains, and looking at it costs you something small you don't notice leaving. The other is gold, and steady, and warm, and the light coming off it feels like being looked at kindly by someone very tired.*

- **Neither orb speaks.** No voices, no bargaining. Both transmit, and what they transmit arrives already inside the person, indistinguishable from their own feelings.
- **Describe the effects, never the source.** If a player asks which feeling comes from which orb: *you can't tell.*
  - **A settled calm.** The hard part is over. The obvious thing is also the good thing. Doing it will feel like setting down something heavy. The room feels **safe** — the most dangerous thing it could feel like.
  - **A wordless, straining push.** Not a shout — the way a shout hurts when someone is stopping you stepping into the road. A pleading ***no***, carrying exhaustion, and the unmistakable sense that whatever is behind it is a *person* who's been alone a very long time.
- **Paths:**
  - If the PCs remember Ilvessa's line → "believe the light" points at the gold orb. Only clue in the room, and only if they earned it in Scene 1.
  - If the PCs attack, free, move, or cut down either orb → same as sitting. Branch B.
  - If Hex hasn't confessed the drider yet → this is his last clean chance.
  - **If a PC (Snacks?) wants the chair for their own reasons** → don't talk them out of it in your own voice. Let the party do it.

## Scene 4 — The Choice

### Branch A — Touch nothing, leave *(the correct play)*

- No fight. No chase. Nothing happens.
- Silent climb, dead deck, over the rail. The Shadow sails on with no wind. The storm closes behind them. **The compass goes inert** the moment they clear the weather.
- They have: their lives, their captain, a dead compass, **no answers.**
- **Play the anticlimax straight.** Do not add a hazard to make it feel earned. Let them sit in *did we just do the right thing, or waste all of that.*

### Branch B — Someone sits

- He lowers himself in almost gently. Two seconds of nothing.

> *Then he is not there. The chair does not move. Nothing falls, nothing cries out — a man simply stops being in a place, and a hundred feet below you every chain in the hull goes slack at once.*

- **Lolth's power is loose, wearing him.** **Seraphel is free** — the gold orb unfolds into a man staring at his own hands.
- The thing wearing Hex's face is unhurried, pleased, uninterested in fighting. *"Thank you"* — his voice, polite, not grateful. Then it steps off the rail, **walks on the water**, and does not look back.
- Run the gauntlet.

---

## The Gauntlet *(Branch B only)*

- **The ship unravels, it doesn't sink.** Deck splits from the inside, mast falls in sections, water erupts instead of rushing, timbers blacken like ash. Slack chains thrashing like live things.
- **Maren cuts the line** the moment she sees Hex step off the wreck. No hesitation. Signal was five minutes; it's been thirty seconds.
- **Get to the rail:** Athletics/Acrobatics to move clean, or just run and take **DC 14 Dex, 2d8 bludgeoning** from debris and falling rigging.
- **Reach the Stormcaller:** swim **DC 14 Athletics**; thrown rope; grab driftwood. Nobody is coming back out for them.
- **In the water:** **DC 13 Survival/Str** to grab something that floats. **2d6 cold, DC 12 Con for half**, repeating. Not a fight — hypothermia. Play it quiet and wrong.
- **The Stormcaller is wrecked** and pulling away — mainmast snapped and hanging, deck scorched, taking water starboard. Night falls. The storm is breaking apart, and so is everything else.
- **Then the gold light.** Seraphel rises out of the water. Doesn't explain, doesn't introduce himself:

> *"I need you to listen very carefully. Don't let go of what you're holding."*

- The water stops being quite so cold where he passes. He **levitates all of them**, driftwood and all, toward the ship's lights. Maren's crew hauls them aboard without asking.
- Last one aboard, **the light goes out.** He's just a man, soaking wet, looking at the sky like he's seeing it for the first time.
- **The storm breaks** — not closes. Wind dies, clouds tear, rain stops mid-drop. Real stars. Far behind them, **the Shadow goes under.**

## Scene 5 — The Answers

### 5A — Xendros *(Branch A)*

- They come home with nothing and go to the one person in Saltmarsh who deals in what she shouldn't. **She already knows.** Has for years.
- Not sorry, and would never have stopped them — she'd have been curious either way. But she likes them and they owe her an item, so she'll talk.
- Give her the full **Truth**, in her voice.
- **On Ilvessa:** precise and unbothered. A drider, one of Lolth's ruined ones, sent out to do the errands the Queen can't. Thirty years is nothing to a creature like that — **Hex wasn't recruited, he was planted, and left to grow.**
- **If Hex still hasn't confessed the drider, Xendros does it for him** — casually, no idea she's detonating anything: *"Well, obviously. Eight legs. You knew that, Captain."* Ugliest available version of the reveal, and a fair one if nobody asked.
- **She does not know about "believe the light."** If a player repeats it, she stops being amused for exactly one moment. Somebody down there broke ranks — most interesting thing she's heard in years.
- **End on Hex's face** when he understands he's been walked toward that ship since he was a boy — and on knowing a man is still down there holding it alone, and the way to help him is the one thing they must never do.

### 5B — Seraphel *(Branch B)*

Give him **The Truth**, roughly in this order:

1. What the purple thing is, and that the sea powers couldn't destroy it.
2. He **volunteered**. Nobody sentenced him. He'd choose it again. He's been awake the entire time.
3. Why the storm — hide it, bar gods, keep it moving.
4. Sallow sat willingly and was taken. Seraphel watched from the inside. He'll say Sallow was a good man and that it made no difference at all.
5. No anchor for a hundred years; he's been slipping the whole time.
6. **She cultivated Hex.** Chart, exile, obsession, blood oath, compass — all of it was road.
7. What's loose now, and that it can't be put back the way it was.

- **On "believe the light":** it stops him cold. Centuries as a sphere with no way to know anyone out in the world ever tried to help — and it was one of *her* creatures. **He'll want to find Ilvessa.** That want should be the first thing he asks them for.
- **The note to play him on:** he's not angry with them. He's *relieved*, and deeply ashamed of being relieved, because his relief cost the world something enormous. He won't say he forgives them, because from his side there's nothing to forgive — and he knows they need to hear it anyway.
- He stays. New ally, and the only one who understands the enemy.
