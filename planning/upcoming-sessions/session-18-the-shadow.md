# Session 18 Plan — The Shadow

**Picking up from:** Session 17 ended with the party at the threshold of Lolth's final chamber. They have the key, they have the door open, and they have not yet touched anything inside. Nothing in the sanctum has been resolved.

**Session goal:** Run the sanctum fight, get the compass, get off the island, and reach the Shadow — where the real story starts.

---

## Reference — what the party already has

Carried forward from Session 17:

- **Silver spider key** — used to open the sealed door.
- **Purple glowing orb** — found in the cavern, purpose still unknown to them.
- **Magical spellbook** — the cultist-scribe's journal. The party has read the last legible entry (see below).
- **+1 sword, extra 1d4 acid damage.**
- **Joss Tagrin** — rescued deckhand off the *Gull's Wager*, last of his crew. Not in fighting shape (speed 15, can't hold a weapon). Whether they brought him along or left him caged is a live thread.
- **Unresolved from the beach:** an orc cultist and a dead human, never explained.

**The journal entry they have already read — this is the load-bearing clue:**

> *"She is not waiting for the curse to break. She is waiting for it to break **twice**. The first time was the captain. The second time will be us — whoever takes the helm next. She wants the chair filled and emptied again. I do not know why. I have stopped asking. The webs answer questions they were not asked, and I would rather not be answered."*

**Lore they did NOT find** (Room 5, the Altar of the Deep Queen — still available if you want to seed it elsewhere, or drop it):

> *Sixty years ago the Spider Queen came to this island on purpose, drawn by the storm and the black ship that sails inside it. The Shadow's curse is older than her claim on the Abyss. Whoever breaks that curse will not destroy what waits aboard the Shadow — they will **free** it. She does not guard the compass to keep the Shadow lost. She guards it to make certain that when the Shadow is found, it is found by the hand she chooses, at the hour she chooses, and no sooner.*

---

## Scene 1 — The Sanctum (Lolth's Final Room)

The sealed door opens into a volcanic cavern, roughly 60 feet wide and 80 feet long, everything slick black rock. Three things dominate the space:

**The two wall crystals** — set into carved sconces on the left and right walls, each about 3 feet tall, pulsing violet, humming just below hearing. These are **binding seals** — they are what keeps the central crystal intact and Lolth imprisoned inside it. As long as both stand, the prison holds.

**The central crystal** — 8 feet tall, a jagged pillar of deep violet growing from the floor at the room's midpoint. Inside it, visible as a shadow pressed against the glass, is Lolth: too many limbs, utterly still. The crystal shows no cracks — it is sealed, and she cannot yet reach through it. The compass is visible as a faint silver glint near the base, suspended inside. **The only way to get it is to break the crystal open.**

**The altar** — at the far end, 60 feet from the door. The compass rests here, magnetically locked in place by the crystal's power — it won't budge until the central crystal shatters and the tether breaks.

**Guarding the room:** Cultist guardians and **2 stone drider statues** flanking the altar — carved black rock, eerily detailed, posed mid-strike. They are easy to dismiss as decoration. They are not. The moment either wall crystal takes damage, both statues crack open and two living Driders tear free, animating simultaneously and attacking.

### The Shape of the Problem

The party cannot simply fight to the altar and grab the compass. It's physically tethered to the central crystal by the binding's power and won't move until the crystal breaks. To break the central crystal, both wall crystals must be destroyed first — they're the seals holding the prison intact.

**The sequence is forced:**
1. Smash both wall crystals.
2. Break the central crystal.
3. Grab the compass (now free on the altar).
4. Get out before Lolth fully manifests.

The party cannot avoid freeing her. That's the trap. That's the gut-punch. **They have to do it anyway.**

### Destroying the Wall Crystals

Each wall crystal has **20 HP** and takes one concentrated action (or weapon strike) to destroy. When a wall crystal is destroyed:
- It explodes in violet shards — creatures within 5 ft take **2d6 force damage**.
- The central crystal shudders. The compass glints brighter inside.
- Lolth's silhouette moves for the first time.

When the **second** wall crystal is destroyed, the central crystal begins to fracture on its own — deep groaning, cracks splitting down all sides, violet light bleeding through the gaps. It will fully shatter in **3 rounds** on its own, or it can be struck to shatter it immediately (one action, any damage). The choice: wait and fight, or shatter it now and start the escape clock.

### Breaking the Central Crystal

The moment the central crystal shatters:

> *It doesn't break — it detonates. Violet shards scatter across the stone. The heartbeat stops. And then the room fills with a sound that is not sound: a pressure behind the eyes, a weight in the chest, a cold that doesn't touch the skin. The compass hits the altar with a hard silver ring. And from the space where the crystal was, something unfolds.*

- All creatures in the room take **3d8 psychic damage** (DC 14 Wis save, half on success).
- The compass is now free on the altar — **one action to grab it**.
- **Lolth begins to emerge.** She needs **1 round** to fully manifest before she can act freely.

**Round 1 after the shatter:** She is rising — a growing darkness at the crystal's former site, limbs unfolding from nothing. She cannot act yet, but the room reacts: webs erupt from the walls, forcing all creatures to make a **DC 13 Str save** or be restrained (one action to break free). Driders gain advantage on all attacks as she feeds them power.

**Round 2+:** She's free. At the start of this round she acts — a lair-action wave targeting the largest cluster of creatures: **4d10 necrotic damage**, DC 15 Con save for half. Then full stat block. The party will not survive a prolonged fight.

### Tactical Shape of the Fight

**Phase 1 — Clear the room:** The cultist guards engage immediately. The stone drider statues flanking the altar look like art. A DC 16 Perception or Arcana check reveals faint warmth and a barely audible heartbeat from inside the stone — but most parties won't look twice. The moment any wall crystal takes damage, both statues erupt: stone shells crack and fall away, and two Driders emerge fully animate, engaging immediately. The party just woke up the altar guards by going for the crystals.

**Phase 2 — The point of no return:** Both wall crystals are down, the central crystal is cracking. Someone decides: wait 3 rounds for it to shatter on its own and use the time to regroup, or shatter it now and start the 1-round escape window immediately.

**Phase 3 — Grab and run:** One PC grabs the compass from the altar. Everyone heads for the door. 2 rounds before Lolth can fully act.

**Optimal run:**
- **Round 1:** Two PCs engage Driders; two sprint for wall crystals.
- **Round 2:** Both wall crystals smashed. Central crystal begins to crack.
- **Round 3:** Central crystal shattered (action). Compass grabbed (action). Party moves toward door.
- **Round 4:** Everyone through the door. Lolth fully manifests to an empty room.

### The Escape

Through the door and down the tunnel with the compass. **Describe this:** the sealed door slams shut — then something hits it from the other side with a force that sends cracks running across the stone. The door holds. The tunnel is quiet.

Then, from the other side, her voice. Not roaring. Not screaming. Just speaking, in Abyssal, slow and even — the voice of someone who is not surprised. Whoever understands Abyssal hears:

*"You are taking it to exactly where I want it to go."*

Then nothing.

Let them run.

## The Truth — DM reference

*None of this is spoken aloud until Scene 5. Read it first; everything downstream depends on it.*

**What the Shadow is.** A jail. The hull is the cell. Chained below the lowest deck is a **piece of Lolth** — what was cut away from her when she took the Abyss. It was given to the sea to drown, because the sea keeps things. The old sea powers could not destroy it, so they chained it into a hull and set it adrift inside a permanent storm that never sits still: a prison that is never in the same place twice, warded specifically against gods.

**Why the storm.** It is the lock. It is also the ward — it bars *her*, not mortals. She cannot cross it herself. She has spent centuries solving that problem, and a mortal captain is the loophole.

**The first captain was the jailer, not the victim.** He wasn't cursed into the chair; he was *made* for it — he was part of the binding itself. When he finally got out of the seat and died three steps from the rail, that was **the first break.** The storm has been thinning ever since.

**The chair is the gate, not the fix.** A new hand on the lock re-keys it. The moment anyone who is not the original jailer sits in that seat, the ward drops and she comes aboard for her missing piece. **That is the second break.** Scene 1's crystal only got her out of her cell — she is loose but caged inside the storm, unable to reach the ship. Filling the chair is what lets her in.

This is what the compass was always for. She needed mortal hands to carry her key through her own ward and sit down. *"You are taking it to exactly where I want it to go."* — she was never talking about the compass. She was talking about a person.

**Why she chose Hex.** Session 16 established that he navigates by a star cluster matching no known constellation, and that he "got the information from someone." That someone was her. He has been steered toward this chair since he was young.

**Consistency check:** the only sanctum document the party has actually read is the scribe's journal — "break twice, first was the captain, the second will be whoever takes the helm next, she wants the chair filled and emptied again." Every beat above is a direct read of that line. The unfound Room 5 altar lore ("the curse is older than her claim on the Abyss") also holds: the severed piece predates her Abyssal claim while still being hers. Both docs stay true whether or not the party ever sees the second one.

## Scene 2 — Off the Island

The chanting has stopped.

Every cultist on the island is dead. Not killed in a fight — the moment she was free she had no further use for them. The party walks back out through a sanctum and a treeline where everyone is simply finished, and the rain that had fallen straight down without pause since they landed **stops** partway to the beach. That silence is worse than the chanting was.

- **She does not chase.** Nothing pursues them. Play the walk out completely quiet and let the players talk themselves into a panic.
- **Joss Tagrin** (if they brought him) is the one who says it out loud: *"Why isn't it raining?"*
- If they left Joss caged, this is where it costs them — the cage is open and empty, and there's no blood.
- Back aboard the Stormcaller, **Maren** wants a heading. The compass answers.

**DM note:** resist any temptation to add a fight here. The party just took the worst risk of the campaign and got away clean, and the wrongness of getting away clean is the scene.

---

## Scene 3 — The Storm Parts

The compass points, and the weather **opens.**

Hex has spent his entire adult life fighting this storm and it has never once let him through. It is letting them through now — a corridor of flat water and dead air running to the horizon, walls of black cloud standing off on either side like something holding a door.

- **Hex is elated.** Let him have it. This is the best day of his life.
- **Maren is not.** She's the one who says the quiet part: *"Six years I've plotted your courses, Captain. Weather doesn't do favors."*
- **Dov** asks whether they're the first people to ever see this. Nobody answers him.
- Then the Shadow: a black-hulled galleon under full sail with no wind to drive it, silent but for rigging. No lights. No crew. Far bigger than it has any right to be.

---

## Scene 4 — Boarding the Shadow

Empty. Silent. A century too old in every detail — rope that should have rotted, brass that should have gone green, a deck worn smooth by feet that aren't there.

**The quarterdeck.** The ship's wheel, and bolted to the deck behind it, a **captain's seat.** Empty. Heavy. Not decorative.

**The remains.** Three steps from the port rail, a dead man. Old bones in a captain's coat.

> *He is not lying the way a man lies when he falls. He is stretched out — reaching, one arm forward, fingers still curled around nothing. Whatever he was carrying is not here. He was crawling toward the rail, and he did not make it.*

**This is Tell #2 and it matters later.** A **DC 15 Insight or Medicine** check reads the body honestly: he wasn't fleeing the ship. He was trying to throw something *overboard* — and he died three steps short. (What he was carrying was the compass's twin. He'd worked out what it was for and was trying to put it in the sea.)

**Below decks.** It gets older and wronger the further down they go, and the ship is bigger inside than out. The compass keeps pulling **downward** — past the hold, past the bilge, past where a ship stops having decks.

**Optional pressure while they descend** (use one or two, keep them cheap):
- Footsteps on the deck above them, moving aft, unhurried.
- A hammock still swinging in a room with no air moving.
- A ship's bell rings once, from a bell that isn't there anymore.

## Scene 5 — The Altar Below the Lowest Deck

*This is the scene. Give it room.*

> *The compass stops pulling. The hold in front of you should not exist — it is longer than the ship is, and the far end has no far end. A stone altar has grown up into the hull's ribs like something the timbers healed around, and chains run out of it into the wood in every direction, hundreds of them, disappearing into the ship itself. At the center of the altar is something your eye will not hold. It is not a spider. It has no shape you can keep for more than a moment, and every moment you try, it costs you something small.*

### It speaks

Not aloud — the way the obsidian mirror looked back at them on the island. It is **patient, warm, and reasonable.** It has had centuries alone to work out exactly what a person needs to hear, and it aims all of that at whoever is standing closest.

It does not threaten. It explains. And what it says is: **sit down, and all of this stops.**

**Voice notes for the DM:** never raise it. It uses names it shouldn't know. It says *we* a lot. It offers Hex his sister's town safe and his name clean, and it isn't lying about wanting to give him that — it simply also wants the chair filled.

### The four revelations

Deliver these in order, from the altar's inscriptions and its own voice:

1. **The Shadow is a jail.** The first captain was its jailer, not its victim — he *was* part of the binding.
2. **The storm is the lock, and the chair winds it.** It has been thinning for a hundred years, since the seat went empty.
3. **What is chained here is a piece of Lolth** — cut away from her when she took the Abyss and given to the sea to drown. This is why the curse is older than her claim on the Abyss and is still hers.
4. **She gave Hex the stars.** The constellation that matches no known chart, the information he "got from someone." He has been walked to this room since he was a young man.

Land #4 last, and land it on Hex, not on the party.

### The three tells — the chair is the trap

The inscriptions genuinely say *fill the chair and the storm holds.* That is **true — for the one who was made for it.** The party's best instinct is the doom, but it is findable, and there are three independent ways in:

| Tell | Check | What it gives |
|---|---|---|
| **The qualifier in the stone** | DC 17 Religion or Investigation | The inscription reads *"the chair shall be filled by the one who was made for it."* **Made.** Not "anyone." Whoever catches this understands that a foreign hand on the lock is not a repair, it's a second cut. |
| **The captain's body** | DC 15 Insight or Medicine, on the quarterdeck | He wasn't fleeing — he was crawling to the rail to throw the compass's twin into the sea. He knew. If they already made this check in Scene 4, it clicks here for free. |
| **The orb** | Automatic, if they bring it within 10 ft | The purple orb from the Lolth cavern goes **cold** and sounds a note only the person holding it hears. The binding recognizes a piece of itself and is *warning them.* This is the tell that requires no roll — it only requires that they brought it and are paying attention. |

**Give them the orb tell freely.** It's the fair-play safety net, and it pays off an item they've been carrying since Session 17 with no idea what it was.

### The orb, and what it means

Held near the altar, the orb is unmistakably **the same working** as the sanctum's binding crystals — same violet, same sub-audible hum. What that proves: **a person is not the only possible anchor.** The seal can be tied to an object.

What it doesn't do: **finish the job tonight.** It's one piece of a larger binding — not enough power, not enough of it, and nobody aboard knows the old work. That is deliberate. It converts tonight's tragedy into an arc: *find the rest, complete the binding, get whoever is in that chair back out of it.*

### Hex breaks

He receives all of this at the same moment the party does, and it takes him apart. The ship he has chased his entire life is a prison. The stars that led him here were a hand on his shoulder the whole way. The obsession that got him exiled, that cost him his sister, that he made the party swear a blood oath over — it was **installed.**

He does not go for the wheel. He asks them what to do — which he has never done, in the entire campaign.

And then, quietly, he makes the case for sitting: *it's my mess. It's my sister's town. I was always going to end up on this ship.*

**Play him honestly.** He is not being manipulated in the obvious way. He genuinely believes sitting saves everyone, and he genuinely wants to be the man who does it. The players have to talk him out of the best thing he has ever tried to do.

## Scene 6 — The Choice, and the Run

Both branches end in a run for the Stormcaller. The difference is how bad.

### Branch A — Nobody sits *(they caught a tell and talked him down)*

The piece in the hull realizes it isn't getting what it wants and stops being reasonable. It **screams for its mother**, and she answers from outside the ward — the storm walls close, the corridor of flat water shuts, and something enormous starts hammering the weather from the far side.

- Run the **short gauntlet** (legs 3–4 only, below). Heavy damage, real saves, no realistic PC death.
- They get off with the compass, the orb, Hex, and their lives.
- **Cost:** the seal is still thinning and the chair is still empty. She will get in eventually. The clock is now the campaign's clock.

### Branch B — Hex sits *(they missed every tell, or they let him)*

He climbs into the seat almost gently. Nothing dramatic happens for about two seconds.

> *And then every chain in the hold goes slack at once.*

The ward drops. **She comes aboard** — not summoned, not manifested, simply *arriving*, the way weather arrives. Run the **full gauntlet.**

**Hex cannot get up.** He is conscious, lucid, and pinned — he can talk and he cannot stand, and he spends his last visible moments telling them to run. The Shadow sails off into the storm with him in the chair and a piece of a goddess whispering three feet beneath him.

**This is the rescue hook.** Complete the orb's binding, anchor the seal to an object, get Hex out of that chair. The party has a reason to come back and a person to come back for.

### The gauntlet

**Lolth is not a fight.** No attack rolls against her, no HP, no initiative slot as a combatant. She is a lair-scale force that acts once per round while the party runs. Do not let anyone roll to hit her; describe attacks glancing off the fact of her.

**Say the clock out loud, in character, before round 1.** Maren, from the Stormcaller: *"Five minutes, then I cut the line — I'm sorry, I'm sorry, five minutes!"* Nobody dies to a rule they weren't told.

**Four legs, one per round, from the bottom hold to the Stormcaller's deck:**

| Leg | The move | Hazard |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — The hold** | Get out of the altar room | Chains whip loose. **DC 14 Dex**, 3d8 bludgeoning on a fail, half on a success. Failure also costs 10 ft. |
| **2 — The lower decks** | Climb back up through a ship that is folding | The stairs are no longer where they were. **DC 15 Wis** or lose a round to a corridor that repeats. Anyone who lingers takes **3d8 psychic**. |
| **3 — The main deck** | Cross open deck under her | Lair action: **4d10 necrotic**, DC 15 Con for half, centered on the largest cluster of creatures. Reward anyone who spreads out. |
| **4 — Over the rail** | Longboat or swim to the Stormcaller | Webbing across the water. **DC 13 Str** or restrained; one action to break free — and that action is a round they do not have. |

**Tuning:**
- Aim for **one PC hitting 0 HP** in a typical run. A downed character costs another PC their movement for a leg to drag them — that is the pressure that makes the clock bite.
- **Death is on the table** for a party that splits, lingers to loot, or leaves someone. It should not be the expected outcome.
- Three players, levels unrecorded in the character files — **check their actual HP before the session and adjust the 3d8/4d10 dice up or down.** The shape matters more than the numbers.
- **Hard line, telegraphed:** anything still aboard the Shadow at the end of leg 4 does not come back.
- Give them outs that cost something: burning the orb buys a full round (and sets the rescue arc back), Hex shouting a route buys 10 ft, dropping the compass into the sea buys… something they will not want to pay.

**The last image.** From the Stormcaller's deck, the storm closing behind them, the Shadow sailing away under full sail with no wind — and a single figure seated behind the wheel, not moving.

## Loose Threads Going Out

- **Hex is in the chair** (Branch B) or **the chair is still empty** (Branch A). Either way the seal is unfinished.
- **The orb** is one piece of a binding. Where are the others? Xendros is owed a magic item and knows old work. The sea elves and Oceanus have a stake in what's chained under that sea.
- **Eda Oweland** has to be told what happened to her brother.
- **Lolth is loose** and no longer caged by the storm in Branch B.
- **Joss Tagrin** — returning him to Saltmarsh, and to whatever's left of the *Gull's Wager's* families.
- **The orc cultist and dead human on the beach** (Session 17) were never explained.
- **The compass** still works. It points at whatever she wants found next.
